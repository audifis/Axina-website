OHADA accounting, in plain words.

The terms you come across in AXINA, at your accountant's or at the bank, explained without jargon.

ABCDEGJLMOPSTV

A

Uniform Act on Accounting Law (AUDCIF)
The OHADA text that sets the rules for accounting and financial reporting in the 17 member countries. The revised SYSCOHADA derives from it.
Credit note
A document that cancels all or part of an invoice already issued. You don't change an invoice: you issue a credit note.

B

Trial balance
The table that lists every account with its debit and credit totals. If the accounts are right, both totals are equal.
Balance sheet
A snapshot of the company on a given date: what it owns (assets) and how that is financed (liabilities and equity).

C

Cash flow from operations (CAF)
The cash the business generates by itself, before paying dividends. Banks look at it to judge the ability to repay.
Year-end close
The moment the year's accounts are closed. After the close, the periods are locked.
Income statement
The film of the year: what the company earned (income) and spent (expenses). The difference is the result.

D

DSF
The statistical and tax return: the annual file a company submits to the authorities, with its financial statements.

E

Accounting entry
The record of a transaction in the accounts, with at least one debit and one credit of the same amount.
Fingerprint (hash)
A string of characters calculated from content. If the content changes by a single character, the fingerprint changes completely. AXINA uses SHA-256.
Financial year
The period the accounts cover, usually twelve months.
Reversal
An entry that cancels a previous entry by swapping its debits and credits. It is the clean way to correct a mistake.

G

General ledger
All the accounts with the detail of every entry that moved them.

J

Journal
The chronological record of entries, often split by type: sales, purchases, bank, miscellaneous.

L

Tax return pack
The set of standard schedules (balance sheet, income statement, notes) attached to the annual return.

M

Mandate
In AXINA, the authorisation a company gives its accounting firm, with a scope and a duration. No mandate, no access.

O

OHADA
The Organisation for the Harmonisation of Business Law in Africa, which brings 17 countries together around common rules, including accounting.
Evidentiary value
The ability of information to be presented and relied on against a third party: a judge, a bank, the authorities. Regular, documented and traceable accounts strengthen it; the judge decides.

P

Supporting document
The document that proves a transaction: invoice, receipt, statement, contract. Every entry should trace back to its document.
SYSCOHADA chart of accounts
The standard list of accounts, by class: 1 long-term funding, 2 fixed assets, 3 inventories, 4 third parties, 5 cash, 6 expenses, 7 income, 8 other expenses and income, 9 commitments and management accounting.

S

DIP sealing
In AXINA, computing an entry's fingerprint, chained to the previous entry's. Changing the past would break the chain.
Revised SYSCOHADA
The accounting system of the OHADA area, revised and applicable to company accounts since 1 January 2018.

T

Output and input VAT
Output VAT is the VAT you charge your customers. Input VAT is the VAT you pay your suppliers. You pay over the difference.

V

Sign-off
The act by which a professional of the firm puts their name to a file. In AXINA, it is named and dated.
